# Notes Along the Way — illustration style

Create an original editorial illustration in fine blue pen-and-ink on warm ivory
paper. Use restrained hatching, natural imperfect lines and
a small number of concrete, recognisable objects. Keep the subject legible at
mobile size, with essential details away from the edges. Avoid readable text,
logos, interface elements, glossy 3D, neon, stock technology symbolism and generic
AI brains. Match the line quality and palette of the supplied notebooks reference,
not its subject or composition. Do not invent archaeological evidence or present
speculative historical scenes as documentary fact. Use source-driven diagrams
and charts when exact quantities or relationships matter.

Follow the composition requirements for the selected canvas and purpose. Article
illustrations and book/collection covers should use most of the canvas, with small
outer margins and enough space between objects to keep their silhouettes clear.
Avoid large blank bands and do not reserve space for title lettering. Keep
essential objects fully visible. The reference provides ink and palette, not a
layout to copy. Inspect mechanical details, attachments and repeated objects
before selecting a candidate; a plausible overall scene can still contain errors.
